About

I am a Ph.D. candidate at Department of Informatics, University of California, Irvine. I am advised by Dr. Anne Marie Piper as a member of Accessibility Research Collective. Outside school, I volunteer with AImpower.org to co-develop more accessible language and communication technologies with and for people who stutter.

Research Interests

I am broadly interested in how to design technologies that make room for human differences. My current focus is to develop “Cripping AI”, a research agenda that positions disability as a critical source of knowledge to interrogate the restrictive assumptions underlying AI development and develop AI systems that support different human bodies and minds.

Across Sign Language AI, Visual AI, and Speech AI, I work with disabled communities, showing how disability knowledge can help advance human-centered AI across the entire development pipeline, including:

  • Problem Formulation, e.g., through analyzing deaf communities' translation work, I reframe machine translation from a task of language conversion to a languaging activity to better account for fluidity in human language use [1].
  • Data Representation, e.g., my disability-led annotation work shows that speakers' lived experiences is a key form of expertise to speech dataset creation [2].
  • Design & Evaluation, e.g., my work on visual AI for blind people shows subjectivity inherent in visual descriptions and limitations in design and evaluation frameworks that privilege sighted perspectives [3, 4].
  • Deployment & Impact, e.g., my ongoing work examines impacts AI brings to high-stakes contexts such as employment.

I have published and co-authored work across top-tier venues in HCI and Responsible AI including ACM CHI, CSCW, FAccT, DIS, and IEEE S&P. I have received recognitions including two Honorable Mention Awards from CHI and two Diversity, Equity and Inclusion (DEI) recognitions from CSCW.
